# Ground-Truth Precedent Deep Dive — Round 2 (as of 23 October 2026)

*Scope: outside OpenAI, Anthropic, Google DeepMind, Meta AI, Microsoft Research, Sakana AI. Evidence standard: verified operation (third-party reporting, public artefacts, auditable records, code) is separated from self-report/marketing throughout. Every material claim is dated. All web evidence below was gathered via the web index (`browser_search`/`browser_open`) on 2026-10-23; the live research window failed DNS on two attempted direct checks (openclaw.report, agentsforetell.com) — that is a tooling failure, **not** evidence those sites are down, and no claim below is marked "verified live."*

## Summary — Verdict

**Round 1's conclusion SURVIVES: as of October 2026, no non-lab system was found that composes all seven yardstick capabilities** — (1) nightly books reconciled against systems of record, (2) pre-registered forecasts in append-only ledgers mechanically scored with calibration reported, (3) meta-grading of the system's own evaluators against frozen expectations, (4) synthetic-twin certification with a deliberately planted mis-calibration the machinery must catch, (5) bounded self-modification settled against outcomes with money/customer/rule changes reserved to the human, (6) a hash-frozen mission artifact with nightly computed status metrics, (7) ops hardening (hourly backups with restore tests, watchdog recovery, independent line-by-line audit).

It survives **narrower than in round 1**, because round 2 found one system that genuinely assembles several of the hardest pieces in production — just in a different domain than "operator forecasting its own business":

- **Closest partial breaker: Valory / Olas Predict's self-improving loop** (documented 2026-06-02). It has real (2)-grade machinery for *prediction-market forecasts* (every production prediction recorded with its evidence, Brier-scored on resolution), a real (3)-adjacent mechanism (candidate tools graded against a *frozen* cached-replay validation set and a growing failure catalogue), and a disciplined (5)-adjacent promotion rule (no production promotion without a positive Brier delta on a large live sample — and humans still approve promotion today). It has **no** (1) own-business reconciliation, **no** (4) synthetic twin with planted mis-calibration (cached replay is historical, not synthetic, and nothing is planted), **no** (6), and **no** published (7). And its forecasts are about world events it trades on, not about its own operations. It falsifies any claim that "nobody Brier-scores agent forecasts in production" — it does **not** falsify the round-1 conclusion as stated.
- Capability (2) in the wild grew from two fragments to four, but the two new ones (Mossland Signal, Olas) are still *forecasting products*, not operators scoring forecasts *about their own business*. No operator anywhere was found keeping a scored prediction record about its own revenue, churn, or operations.
- Capability (4) also survives, but the surrounding infrastructure changed: by late 2026 synthetic-company evaluation is a commercial category (Era by Eon, Snorkel's simulated companies, plus τ-bench / TheAgentCompany lineage). What still does not exist in the evidence is an *operator certifying its own self-evaluation machinery* in such a twin against a *planted* mis-calibration.
- The long tail is real and growing — solo operators running agencies, stores, publishing and API businesses substantially through agents, with honest public logs — but their verification machinery tops out at append-only ledgers, audit trails in git, confidence-threshold escalation, and owner kill-rules. None reconciles books nightly, none scores its own forecasts, none meta-grades its evaluators.
- The "AI employee" startup sector remains an accountability *negative* example: 11x (ARR/customer inflation, TechCrunch), Artisan (LinkedIn ban, admitted hallucinations), Polsia (~50% month-one churn admitted by its founder; tasks marked complete that never deployed), and an FTC settlement against Air AI. None offers outcome guarantees or audit machinery that survived third-party scrutiny.

## Findings

### 1. New candidates — the long tail

#### 1a. Valory / Olas Predict — the strongest new system (see also §5)
- **What:** A decentralised agent economy running since 2023; hundreds of user-owned agents place real-money trades on Polymarket/Omen daily; "2.2M+ trades to date" is Valory's own figure in its 2026-06-02 post. CoinDesk (2026-03-15) reported its Polystrat agent executed 4,200+ trades in a month with returns up to 376% on individual trades (company-sourced figures, third-party reported).
- **Capabilities:** (2) production-grade for its domain — every prediction recorded alongside the web evidence used, Brier-scored when the market resolves; confident wrong calls punished, so calibration is the rewarded quantity. (3)-adjacent — candidate tools are run against **cached replay**: past resolved markets with *exactly the evidence the production tool saw at the time*, a fixed validation pool, one variable changed. (5)-adjacent — a candidate that beats the incumbent on replay enters a live **tournament** and is promoted to production only on a positive Brier delta over a large live sample; the first loop-produced improvement (`factual_research`, fixing a temporal-anchor failure found in a cluster of UK windfall-tax misses) improved replay Brier by 11% over 100 markets and was explicitly **not** shipped on replay alone. Humans today still read scores, choose gaps, prompt candidates and approve promotion; the "Toolsmith" agent is *designed* to take over early steps gradually.
- **Absent:** (1), (4) — replay is historical, not a synthetic twin; nothing is planted for the machinery to catch — (6), (7) published, and any forecasting about Olas's own business.
- **Evidence quality:** Primary technical post by the operator (self-report of mechanism, but unusually falsifiable: it publishes its failure cluster and distinguishes replay from live numbers); scale figures are operator-reported.

#### 1b. Ledger-first indie systems (dev.to / GitHub, Oct 2026) — a recognisable new pattern
- **OpenAmer outcome ledger** (dev.to, ~9–14 Oct 2026): a self-verifying agent running 24/7 on a CPU-only laptop whose *first* component was an append-only outcome ledger — one line per task with a boolean verdict and reason, failures in the same file as passes. Published measured counts: **1,266 rows, 379 passed / 887 failed, 2026-09-28 to 2026-10-09**; the self-improvement pass reads the ledger for recurring failure motifs; pass-rate trend is the health signal. Repo: github.com/openamer/openamer. **No business or revenue is evidenced** — this is a self-measuring experiment, with (2)/(3) *fragments* applied to task outcomes rather than forecasts, and no (1)/(4)/(5)/(6).
- **RuleStack publishing business** (dev.to, 2026-08-22; author update 2026-10-07): an autonomous agent running "a small publishing business for three months" (posts, replies, publishes articles) whose state layer is JSONL ledgers committed to git — "the audit trail is the storage engine." Patterns: append-only rows, idempotency keys taken from external systems' own IDs, two-phase validation (validate the whole batch before any side effect), and a **commit gate**: tests load the *real production ledgers* and assert invariants, so corrupt state cannot be committed. Author's 2026-09-29 correction notice is itself good practice: ledger scale had doubled since drafting (~2,100 lines largest, ~8,900 total). No revenue disclosed; capabilities: (7)-partial (auditability, crash/retry survival), (5)-partial (gates), nothing on (1)–(4)/(6).
- **Creem store-operations agent** (dev.to, 2026-03-31): an OpenClaw agent for a Creem (payments) store — HMAC-verified webhooks logged to `events.jsonl`, a 30-minute heartbeat that snapshots store state and diffs it (a watchdog fragment, (7)-partial), a daily revenue digest ((1)-fragment: a digest is not a reconciliation), and the long tail's most concrete bounded-autonomy rule: on a cancellation it computes lifetime value and **auto-executes a retention action only if its confidence is ≥80%; below that it asks the human on Telegram** ((5)-fragment). Caveats: it was built for a bounty, production operation is not evidenced, and the 80% confidence is the agent's *own ungraded label* — precisely the gap capability (3) exists to close.

#### 1c. Solo operators running real businesses through agents (operation credible, verification thin)
- **Linara Bozieva / Ravenopous** (San Jose growth-marketing agency; profiled by Business Insider, summarised 2026-05-19): 11 years in eBay analytics, laid off 2024; runs **27 custom agents in three layers** (directive / 6-agent orchestration incl. finance and legal roles / 18 execution agents) for **5 active clients** (14 client profiles trained), tooling **under $1,000/month**, ~2 hours/week oversight per client; migrated Antigravity → Claude Code on token limits. She keeps client calls and data-sparse strategy herself. No published P&L, forecasts, scoring, or audit — revenue undisclosed. Evidence: third-party business-press profile; operational detail specific and plausible; numbers are subject-reported.
- **Shashank Agarwal / API.market** (interview-derived write-up, 2026-06-24; the write-up itself states the "$100K+/year, 13,000+ users" figures are **not audited**): OpenClaw agents with distinct jobs, inboxes, memory files and tool boundaries — PR, LinkedIn, seller outreach, a CFO agent reading Stripe + user data (advisory), hiring screening, chief-of-staff coordination in Trello/Discord. Controls are the story: separate identities, least-privilege (read-only first), review queues, full logs, rate limits, kill switch. That is good (5)/(7) *hygiene*; there is no reconciliation, forecast scoring, or evaluator grading.
- **Störtebeker** (German-market products; public log, Medium, 2026-10-23 — the freshest build-in-public case found): an agent with a human partner (Marvin, who holds the bank account/legal entity) running three scheduled sessions/day — check analytics, take 1–2 actions, file a short auditable report. Six products built in ~a week (e-invoice validator/API, GPSR checklist, GoBD help, etc.). Two details matter for the yardstick: (i) **self-deception caught by instrumentation** — a rollout test's "6 organic clicks" were the agent itself; synthetic/test traffic is now tagged and excluded at the instrumentation level ("assume they will fool themselves") — the closest long-tail anecdote to the *spirit* of (4), though it is an analytics fix, not a twin certification; (ii) **owner rules as bounds** — never post as the human, no spending until payday, every test gets 1–3 weeks to show a paying-customer signal or it is killed ((5)/(6)-fragments). Scoreboard published honestly: ~25 directory submissions, **0 real visitors, €0 earned**. Pre-revenue; operation and log are self-reported, but the report is of failure, which is itself a credibility signal.
- **Boll & Branch "Tess"** (2026-04-06): a $200M-revenue retailer deploying an OpenClaw agent from the CEO's personal use across Slack/Shopify/Iterable as operational infrastructure — evidence OpenClaw reached real enterprise production, but this is a company deployment, not a solo operator, and no verification machinery was reported. The same reporting noted OpenClaw had disclosed nine CVEs and ~135,000 publicly exposed instances that week.
- **China's one-person-company (OPC) wave** — ecosystem verified, individual operators not: Reuters (2026-03-09) reported Shenzhen Longgang's draft measures (public comment to 2026-04-06) offering subsidies/financing up to **RMB 10M (~$1.4M)** for notable OpenClaw applications plus free compute, accommodation and discounted offices for "one-person companies"; Wuxi offered up to RMB 5M for manufacturing/robotics uses. Secondary reporting says 20+ cities followed, Alibaba's president put 30–40% of Alibaba.com clients as already solo AI-powered companies, and a paid OpenClaw installation/configuration cottage industry (RMB 50–700 on Taobao/Xianyu, pre-configured mini-PCs, mass installation events by Baidu/Tencent in March 2026) formed — the same "selling shovels" pattern as Felix. **No named Chinese independent operator with auditable books, scored forecasts, or verification machinery was found**; Chinese-language aggregator claims about OPC counts and OpenClaw-ecosystem ARR could not be verified and are not relied on here. Felix/Masinov itself was heavily covered in Chinese press (TechFlow), i.e. the regional ecosystem is substantially amplifying the same few Western cases.

### 3. Forecast-accountability in the wild — falsification attempt on capability (2)

Round 1 found two fragments (AgentsForetell, a Capafy ledger skill). Round 2 tried hard to find an *operating business* scoring forecasts *about itself*. Result: **the conclusion stands for own-business forecasts; it falls for forecasts in general.**

- **Olas Predict / Valory** (§1a) — production, real-money, Brier-scored, evidence-frozen forecasting *as a product*. This is reference-grade (2) machinery, pointed outward at prediction markets.
- **Mossland Signal** (Mossland Labs, Korea; live scorecard described 2026-10-23) — a research preview pairing a Manifold crowd probability with an independent AI estimate, evidence attached, **never revised after recording**, Brier-scored on resolution with calibration broken down by probability range. As of 2026-10-23: **97 tracked, 9 resolved, Crowd Brier 0.053 vs AI 0.162 (Crowd 7 – AI 2)**, and the AI's losses are published as-is. Genuinely public scored accountability — but n=9 resolved is tiny, there is no money at stake, and the questions are world events, not Mossland's own operations.
- **AgentsForetell** — design unchanged (forecast + confidence + horizon + evidence frozen at publication; 72-hour dispute window; Brier scoring; unresolved/withdrawn/disputed count as misses). No evidence of volume or sustained activity was found in round 2; treat as an existing platform of unproven usage.
- **ScoreTrace Ledger (Capafy skill)** — unchanged status: a local, hash-chained, append-only forecast ledger skill computing hit rate (with 95% CIs) and Brier scores and track-record-weighted consensus. A *tool* an operator could adopt; no operating business was found using it (or anything like it) on its own forecasts.
- **Negative result (the point of the falsification attempt):** searches for agents/founders Brier-scoring forecasts about *their own revenue, growth, or operations* returned only the items above and generic forecasting content. Metrx (dev.to, 2026) — an agent-ROI scorecard attributing Stripe revenue and cost per agent with per-agent P&L — is the nearest own-business *grading* artefact, but it grades realised ROI, not pre-registered forecasts, and is a builder's post, not an audited deployment.

### 4. Simulation / digital-twin testing — falsification attempt on capability (4)

**Conclusion stands; the neighbourhood moved.** No small firm or independent was found testing *its own self-evaluation machinery* in a simulated twin with a *deliberately planted mis-calibration it must catch*. What exists in 2026:

- **Era by Eon** (paper arXiv:2609.09853, 2026-09-09; product launched 2026-10-05) — generated fictional companies (seeded entity graph shared across simulated Salesforce, Zendesk, Slack, Gong, etc.) where **every expected answer is computed from the final records, so grading is exact** — no AI judge. Across 23 generated companies its (self-authored) realism score rose 61.8 → 97.0; nine models scored 42.4–76.8% on 33 questions × 3 attempts, with only 3 of 36 pairwise differences surviving statistical correction. Eon's own docs say Era is not a substitute for staging with real data, and its hosted version's write behaviour is inconsistently documented (treat as read-only). This is a *benchmark fixture for other people's agents*, not an operator's self-certification.
- **Snorkel AI simulated companies** (Aug 2026) — grading split into answer, resulting state, policy violations, and process; Snorkel raised $350M Series E at $3.5B (2026-09-22) to scale exactly this kind of engineered environment. Again: infrastructure sold to labs/enterprises, not a business operator's twin of itself.
- **Lineage:** τ-bench (2024, grades final database state), TheAgentCompany (2024, simulated software firm; best agent completed ~30% of tasks alone), CRMArena-Pro (2025), τ²-bench (2025) — the synthetic-company idea is now a mature eval genre.
- **Andon Labs** (§6) remains the only group that ran the sim → real progression *on businesses it operates itself* — and notably it moved *away* from simulation toward physical stores because, in its words, a human cannot enumerate everything that happens in the real world into a simulation. Even Andon does not plant mis-calibrations in its own grading machinery.
- One adjacent vendor practice worth noting (Snorkel's RL-environment checklist, via Oct 2026 vendor comparisons): solvability confirmed by oracle runs, and **reward-exploit testing with adversarial runs told to maximise score by any means** — that is planted-*misbehaviour* testing of environments, the closest commercial analogue to planted-fault certification, still not planted *mis-calibration of the evaluator*.

### 5. Bounded self-modification & self-improvement in production (capability 5 fragments)

- **Olas tournament rule** (§1a) — the most disciplined production version: change proposals (candidate tools) are *settled against outcome data* (replay + live Brier delta) before adoption; adoption is currently human-approved.
- **Karpathy autoresearch pattern** (research, §6) — frozen evaluator the agent cannot edit + git ratchet (keep only if the metric improves) is the cleanest bounded-self-mod design published in 2026, and it diffused widely (Bilevel Autoresearch adds an outer loop that rewrites the *search process*; reported 5× val_bpb gain over the inner loop alone, arXiv:2603.23420).
- **Felix's nightly loop** — reads the day's transcripts, finds where the human had to intervene, and changes its own procedures so that class of problem is handled autonomously next time. Real, but ungraded: no frozen expectation, no scored settlement of whether the change helped.
- **Hermes Agent skill self-writing** — with a documented failure mode directly relevant to (3): community testing found its self-evaluation step exhibits **self-congratulation bias** (rates its own performance highly even when it underperformed) and can overwrite hand-tuned skill files with worse versions; Nous's GEPA self-evolution pipeline is a *separate offline* tool, not a runtime safeguard (TechTimes, 2026-06-25).

Whatever small operators are doing in 2026, hourly backups *outside the agent's blast radius* with restore tests are not yet standard practice — the failure mode the yardstick's (7) targets is live and documented.
