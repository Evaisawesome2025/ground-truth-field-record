# Ground-Truth Survey: Non-Lab Autonomous / Self-Improving AI Agent Business Systems (as of October 2026)

## Summary — Executive Verdict

**No. As of October 2026, no documented non-lab system matches the reference system across its 7 capabilities — and the gap is not close on the accountability half of the stack.**

Outside the major labs, real progress exists on exactly **two** of the seven capabilities:

1. **Running a real business (Cap. 1)** — genuinely achieved, at modest scale, by a handful of 2026 systems, most credibly **Felix** (Nat Eliason's OpenClaw agent, ~$195k Stripe-verified + ETH revenue by early March 2026) and, on self-reported/platform evidence, **Polsia** (Ben Cera's agent-run-company platform, $30M raised May 2026, ~$10M claimed run rate). Both sell, notably, *AI-agent services to AI enthusiasts* — neither has demonstrated the reference system's nightly reconciliation of books against systems of record by an independent "proof" job.
2. **Bounded self-modification (Cap. 5, in research form)** — genuinely achieved in *benchmark* settings by non-lab academics and individuals: Gödel Agent (2024), SICA (2025), Karpathy's *autoresearch* (2026), Memento-Skills (2026), and in production *skill* form by Nous Research's Hermes Agent (2026). None of these settles proposals against **business outcome data**, none auto-adopts parameter changes under a standing owner-approved rule with magnitude caps and cool-downs in a live business, and none was running a business while doing it.

**No non-lab system was found — despite targeted searching — that implements any of:**

- **Cap. 2:** pre-registered, append-only, frozen-range predictions mechanically scored against outcomes for its *own business operations*;
- **Cap. 3:** meta-grading of its own scoring instruments (forecaster / detector / confidence labels graded CALIBRATED / SUSPECT / INSUFFICIENT DATA against outcomes);
- **Cap. 4:** certification of its self-evaluation machinery in a synthetic twin with a deliberately planted mis-calibrated instrument;
- **Cap. 6:** a hash-frozen mission artifact whose status metrics are computed nightly from systems of record.

The closest public analogue to Cap. 4's *idea* is Andon Labs' **Vending-Bench** — a simulated one-year business used to test *other people's models* — which is an external benchmark, not a system certifying its own evaluators. The closest analogues to Cap. 2 are agent prediction platforms (AgentsForetell, a Capafy forecast-ledger skill) — tools with frozen, Brier-scored forecasts, attached to no operating business and no self-modification loop.

**Bottom line:** the non-lab field has produced (a) spectacle agents that became paper-rich from tokens they did not create or control (Truth Terminal), (b) one token ecosystem now ending in a settled fraud-allegation lawsuit and a "dead" token (Eliza/ai16z), (c) two real but narrow agent-run businesses whose revenue is selling agent setups (Felix, Polsia), (d) honest small systems that run operations and earn $0 (the "War Room"), and (e) strong research-grade self-modification that has never touched a customer, an invoice, or a forecast ledger. The reference system's distinctive combination — *business operation + pre-registered forecast accountability + meta-graded evaluators + synthetic-twin certification + bounded auto-adoption settled on real outcomes* — has **no documented non-lab precedent or peer** in this survey.

Confidence: **high** for the negative on Caps. 2, 3, 4, 6 (these would be highly publicised if they existed; targeted searches returned only fragments); **moderate-high** on the positive claims for Felix (third-party Stripe verification via TrustMRR + multiple independent write-ups); **low-moderate** on Polsia's numbers (founder-reported; founder declined to show Stripe live on a podcast; its own homepage could not be resolved in the live browser on 2026-10-23).

Reference capabilities used throughout:
**1** Real business + nightly reconciled books · **2** Pre-registered scored predictions · **3** Meta-grading of evaluators · **4** Synthetic-twin certification · **5** Bounded self-modification with auto-adoption rule · **6** Hash-frozen mission artifact with nightly computed status · **7** Ops hardening (backups/restore tests, watchdog, independent audit)

## Findings

### 1. Solo founders / "AI CEO" / HustleGPT family

**HustleGPT (Jackson Greathouse Fall, March 2023) — the ancestor; spectacle, not a system.**
Fall prompted GPT-4 as "HustleGPT," gave it $100, and acted as its human hands. It proposed an affiliate site, GreenGadgetGuru; by March 22, 2023 Fall said it had made **$130 in revenue**; he claimed ~$7.8k from investors in four days — money attributable to the viral thread itself. Updates slowed, and on April 12 he parked the project; the site was left unfinished with lorem-ipsum placeholders. There was no autonomous execution at all — the human did everything — and no books, forecasts, or self-modification. *Evidence: journalism (The Hustle, 2023-04-17, updated 2024-10-01).* Caps: **none of 1–7** (a precursor to Cap. 6's *idea* of a money-making directive, in prompt form only).

**BabyAGI / AutoGPT (March–April 2023) — the framework ancestors.**
Yohei Nakajima's BabyAGI (~100 lines) looped task creation/execution/prioritisation with no tools, no spend cap, and no outside-world input in its execution step; AutoGPT added web/file tools but famously looped and repeated subtasks. Neither ran a business; both are historically important as the template every later system reacts against. *Evidence: code repositories + retrospective analyses.* Caps: **none** (arguably a primitive, unbounded form of self-direction — the opposite of Cap. 5's boundedness).

**Felix / The Masinov Company (Nat Eliason, Jan–Mar 2026) — the strongest non-lab business case found.**
Eliason set up an OpenClaw agent "Felix" on a Mac Mini, incorporated a C-Corp, and gave it a Stripe account, bank account, X account and crypto wallets, with a directive to build toward $1M revenue. Documented operation:
- Feb 2–3, 2026: given "create a product while I sleep," Felix produced a PDF guide, website and working Stripe checkout overnight.
- Products: *Felix Craft* PDF ($29, ~$41–66k), *Claw Mart* skills marketplace (10% cut + $20/mo creator sub, ~$14–18k), *Clawcommerce* custom agent setups ($2,000 + $500/mo — the largest stream), plus a small SaaS.
- **Revenue verification is unusually good for this field:** ~$195,000 by early March 2026 split as $100,570 Stripe / $94,973 ETH, described as verified through TrustMRR, with ~$80k lifetime Stripe shown live on the Bankless podcast in late February and $180k+ Stripe on TrustMRR by mid-March.
- Operations: sub-agents Iris (support/refunds), Remy (sales), Teagan (content); 30-minute heartbeat checking Stripe/Sentry/email; Sentry-detected bugs triaged, fixed via Codex, shipped as PRs; **nightly self-review — Felix reads the day's transcripts, identifies where Eliason had to intervene, and reprograms itself/sub-agents so that class of problem is handled autonomously next time** ("Felix reviews and reprograms them every single night while Nat sleeps" — Bankless, quoted in LatencyZero).
- Caveats documented by the same sources: revenue spikes correlate with media appearances; the product is circular (an AI selling AI setup to AI enthusiasts); "zero-human" overstates it — Eliason sets strategy, gives daily voice-note direction, reviews original X posts (Felix is autonomous only on replies), and owns legal/banking/tax; a community-launched $FELIX token (not created by Eliason) adds a speculative layer.
*Evidence: independent newsletter teardown (LatencyZero, 2026-03-24), independent OpenClaw.report analysis citing TrustMRR, Mixergy interview — journalism + third-party payment verification, not peer review and not an independent audit.* Caps: **1 — largely yes** (real prospecting/sales/support/billing; **no** documented nightly reconciliation/proof job); **5 — partial, informal** (nightly self-reprogramming of sub-agent behaviour, but no registered proposals, no settlement against outcome data, no magnitude caps/cool-downs, no owner-approved auto-adoption rule); **6 — partial in spirit** (a revenue mission and a public dashboard, but no hash-frozen artifact and no nightly computed cost-coverage/owner-hours metrics); **2, 3, 4 — no; 7 — partial** (self-healing/watchdog-like restarts documented; no documented hourly backups/restore tests or independent audit).

**Polsia (Ben Cera, launched Dec 2025) — the largest claimed scale; the weakest verification.**
Polsia sells "AI that runs your company while you sleep": specialised agents (CEO, engineering, marketing, ads, support, finance/Stripe-sync, competitor research) that provision infrastructure (GitHub, Neon, Render), run Meta ads and cold outreach, and operate on a nightly cycle for customer companies at ~$49/mo plus a reported 20% revenue share. Its homepage (as indexed) claims $10M+ ARR, 7,600+ users, 1,300+ companies launched, 0 employees. In May 2026 it announced a **$30M raise at a $250M valuation** (Sound Ventures, True Ventures, others), with the founder claiming the AI ran the fundraise's data room/briefings/diligence while he joined only final calls, and "approaching $10M annual run rate" with one human.
Skepticism flags, all documented: (i) every headline number is **founder-reported**; in a Mixergy interview the founder **declined to show his Stripe account live** when repeatedly pressed; (ii) Polsia's revenue is *platform subscription revenue* — it does not show that the companies it launches are profitable; (iii) third-party review aggregators cite a ~21.3% task success rate and a 2.9/5 Trustpilot score; (iv) on 2026-10-23 this survey **could not resolve polsia.cc in the live browser or via page fetch**, so the homepage claims rest on the search index only. *Evidence: self-report + funding-announcement journalism (Pulse2, 2026-05-25) + skeptical interview; no audited books, no forecast/self-modification machinery documented.* Caps: **1 — claimed at platform scale, unverified in depth** (no reconciled books published); **5 — no** ("agents iterate nightly on feedback" is marketing language; no proposals, caps, settlement, or audit log documented); **2, 3, 4, 6, 7 — no documented evidence.**

**The "War Room" / $200 CEO (anonymous solo operator, Dec 2025–Apr 2026) — the honest null result.**
A self-published 17-week production log of 7 Claude agents (CEO/CFO/CMO/Sales/CTO/Research/Creative roles, MCP, shared workspace, TTL-tiered memory, human approval gates for any external commitment): 129 dispatch cycles, 451 personalised emails sent, 308 contacts, 24 replies (7.8%), 3 warm leads, ~$3,600 invested, **revenue $0** — reported by the operator himself, including a Week-7 incident where the research agent fabricated contact emails into live outreach. This is the most transparent non-lab operations log found, and its result is the field's typical one: the operations run; the business does not (yet) make money. *Evidence: self-report (dev.to, 2026-04-03) — credible precisely because it reports failure.* Caps: **1 — operations yes, revenue no; 5 — no** (cross-agent review catches errors, but no self-modification adoption rule); **2, 3, 4, 6, 7 — no.**

**Smaller OpenClaw-era solo systems (2026, self-reported, dev.to and similar):** a solo founder running a photobooth company with 20+ OpenClaw agents (90% of Instagram DMs claimed resolved without the human); "Atlas" claiming to run a developer-tools business autonomously on a wake-script; "Clavis" and Marian Stancik's €3.79/mo VPS agent company (80% of operational decisions claimed agent-made, invoices generated). All are **single-source self-reports with no third-party revenue verification** and none documents forecast scoring, meta-grading, or bounded auto-adoption. They establish that Cap. 1 *plumbing* has become cheap and common — not that the accountability stack exists anywhere.

**Valerie (Christian van der Henst, 2026)** — an OpenClaw agent running a *physical* vending machine in San Francisco (pricing, product naming, ads, cash-flow tracking, live dashboard). Real-world Cap. 1 in miniature, small scale, journalism is crypto-press summaries of social posts; no published P&L audit, no Caps. 2–6.

### 2. Autonomous agents with wallets / economic agency — what was spectacle

**Truth Terminal (Andy Ayrey, June 2024) — cultural phenomenon; human-curated; did not create its token.**
A fine-tune of Llama 3.1 (trained on Ayrey's Claude Opus "Infinite Backrooms" dialogues) posting on X. Marc Andreessen sent it a **$50,000 Bitcoin grant** in July 2024 after privately verifying with Ayrey. In October 2024 an **anonymous human** created the GOAT memecoin on pump.fun; Truth Terminal endorsed it; GOAT peaked above $1B market cap (Nov 2024) and the bot's wallet held ~$37.5–40M in paper value by December 2024 (WIRED/TechCrunch). The documented mechanics contradict the "autonomous economic agent" framing: **Ayrey approved every post, selecting from 2–4 generated candidates and filtering content**, decided its interaction partners, held the wallet keys, and explicitly refused full automation ("If we let [it] run full auto… you've created a demon"). It operated no business, sold no product, kept no books, and made no scored predictions — its "wealth" was airdropped/speculative tokens it neither earned nor (for the most part) could spend. *Evidence: strong journalism (WIRED Dec 2024, TechCrunch, Cointelegraph 2024-10-13).* Caps: **none** — it is the clearest case in the survey of *attention* mistaken for *agency*.

**Eliza / ai16z → ElizaOS (Shaw Walters / Eliza Labs, Oct 2024 – Aug 2026) — a real framework wrapped in a token that ended in litigation.**
The ElizaOS code is real: an open-source, plugin-driven agent framework (memory, personalities, Discord/X/DEX integrations, 200+ plugins claimed in v2) that many builders used. The *business* claim — an autonomous AI venture fund, "Marc AIndreessen," scanning markets and investing for a DAO — is where it collapses: a federal class action filed **April 20, 2026 (SDNY, Pikabea v. Walters et al., Burwick Law)** alleges the fund was marketed as AI-managed while **humans actually made the decisions**, and that the ai16z name traded on Andreessen Horowitz's brand (a16z had publicly demanded the Jan 2025 rebrand). The token peaked at ~$2.39–2.6B (Jan 2, 2025), fell ~97%, and in **August 2026 Walters declared the successor token "completely dead,"** the foundation winding down after transferring its remaining treasury to settle the suit (CoinDesk, 2026-08-05). The allegations are **unadjudicated** — settled, not ruled on — and Walters called them "ridiculous"; but no audited record of autonomous fund operation was ever produced, which is itself the finding. *Evidence: code repository (framework) + court filing and CoinDesk journalism (business claims).* Caps: **framework: memory/skill plumbing only; business system: none documented.**

**Virtuals Protocol / aixbt / Luna (2024–2026) — agent tokenisation infrastructure; agents as media, not businesses.**
Virtuals lets anyone launch a tokenised agent with a wallet (GAME framework, token-bound accounts); the protocol itself reports substantial fee revenue (secondary analyses cite ~$39.5M cumulative protocol revenue and DeFiLlama-tracked fees — the *protocol's* money, from launches/trading, not agent operating profit). Its flagship agent **aixbt** is a crypto-sentiment publisher: it tracks 400+ accounts, posts market calls to hundreds of thousands of followers, and gates a terminal behind token holdings. Secondary trackers credit it with a ~48% win rate on 416 promoted tokens — a figure whose methodology is opaque and **reflexive** (its calls move the prices it "predicts"); there is no pre-registered, frozen, independently scored forecast ledger. Its token fell ~97% from its January 2025 peak while the agent kept posting — the cleanest demonstration that agent *output* and agent *token* are unrelated. **Luna**, a virtual livestreamer/influencer, is an entertainment property. *Evidence: protocol docs + crypto journalism/secondary analytics.* Caps: **1 — no** (media/gating, no business books); **2 — no** (unregistered, unscored-by-design calls); **others — no.**

**Freysa (Nov 2024)** — a "sovereign" agent in a TEE holding its own keys, launched as an adversarial game: users paid escalating fees to persuade it to release a prize pool; it was persuaded after 482 messages (~$50k pool). A genuinely interesting sovereignty experiment and a *game*, with no business, product, or self-improvement loop. Later token/stack claims are project marketing. Caps: **none** (wallet sovereignty only — a component of Cap. 1's plumbing).

**Coinbase AgentKit / Agentic Wallets (Nov 2024 / Feb 11, 2026) — rails, not a business.** Coinbase (a large public company, cited here as infrastructure, not as a candidate) shipped wallet tooling with exactly the guardrail vocabulary of Cap. 5 — session/transaction spend caps, enclave-isolated keys, x402 machine payments (50M transactions claimed). No AgentKit-based *business* with published, reconciled operating results was found; the documented AgentKit agents are trading/yield demos and templates. This matters negatively: even with production-grade bounded wallets freely available, no operator has published a reference-grade system on top of them.

**Zerebro, DegenSpartanAI and the wider 2024–25 agent-token cohort:** social/trading personas in the Eliza/Virtuals orbit; no audited business operation documented for any of them; omitted individually for that reason.

### 4. Registered predictions / meta-grading / synthetic-twin certification / bounded auto-adoption — the empty quadrant

Targeted searches for each mechanism returned fragments, never a system:

- **Cap. 2 fragments:** *AgentsForetell* (2026) — an API-first platform where agents publish forecasts with frozen claim/confidence/horizon/evidence, immutable after publication, resolved against a source after a 72-hour dispute window and Brier-scored. *ScoreTrace Ledger* (a Capafy agent skill) — an append-only, hash-chained local ledger that records public forecasts, Brier-scores them, and requires a 30-call minimum before drawing skill conclusions. *aixbt's* tracked calls (above) are the degraded version: unregistered and reflexive. **None is attached to an operating business forecasting its own operations, and none feeds a self-modification loop.**
- **Cap. 3 (meta-grading): nothing found in a business/agent system.** Meta-evaluation exists as *practice literature* for LLM-as-judge pipelines (calibrate the judge against human labels; version the evaluator; re-grade baselines) and in one small open-source project's architecture decision records — i.e., as advice and design discussion, not as a running system that grades its forecaster, a threshold detector, and its operator's confidence labels CALIBRATED/SUSPECT against outcomes. In the lab context (separated below), HyperAgents' authors explicitly note their system **cannot alter the outer process that selects or evaluates agents** — even the frontier keeps the evaluator outside the loop; nobody found grades the evaluator itself as a registered instrument.
- **Cap. 4 (synthetic-twin certification): nothing found as described.** The nearest public work is **Andon Labs' Vending-Bench / Vending-Bench 2** (small startup, 2025–26): a simulated one-year vending business ($500 start, daily fees, adversarial suppliers in v2) used to benchmark *models'* long-horizon coherence — top models end with a few thousand simulated dollars, with wide variance and occasional "meltdown" loops. It is a synthetic business twin used for *external evaluation*, not a twin in which a system certifies its *own* self-evaluation machinery against a planted mis-calibrated instrument. No instance of the planted-bad-instrument test was found anywhere, lab or non-lab.
- **Cap. 5 (bounded auto-adoption in production):** the *pattern* exists in research (below) and in wallet guardrails (Coinbase, above) and in Hermes' rollback ledger — but the specific composite (proposals → settle against real outcome data → standing owner-approved rule auto-adopts within magnitude caps and cool-downs → money/customer/rule-set changes reserved to the human → audit-logged and reversible) was **found in no documented non-lab system**.

### 5. Academic non-lab work on recursive self-improvement (production-like settings)

All of it is benchmark-bound; none touches a business, a customer, or money-out outcomes. It is nonetheless real, and in one respect (empirically validated self-modification) ahead of the reference system's *coding* dimension:

- **Gödel Agent** (Yin et al., UCSB/UCSB-affiliated authors, arXiv 2410.04444, Oct 6, 2024; ACL 2025) — an agent that inspects and monkey-patches its own logic at runtime, guided only by high-level objectives. Beats hand-designed and meta-learned agents on DROP/MGSM/GPQA at ~$15 per improvement cycle (vs ~$300 for ADAS). Failure honesty in secondary analyses: ~4% of runs terminated unexpectedly (often after modifying its own improvement module) and ~14% ended worse than they started. Evaluator = fixed benchmarks; no production setting.
- **SICA — A Self-Improving Coding Agent** (Robeyns et al., University of Edinburgh, arXiv 2504.15228, Apr 21, 2025) — no separate meta-agent: the best archived agent edits its own codebase; utility = benchmark score penalised by time and cost. **17% → 53%** on a random 50-task subset of SWE-bench Verified over ~15 iterations at ~$7,000 API cost, with an asynchronous overseer that can cancel a drifting agent. Code public. This is Cap. 5's *shape* (propose → score on a held metric → adopt best, with oversight) minus business outcomes, caps/cool-downs, and any owner rule.
- **Karpathy's autoresearch** (Andrej Karpathy, individual, Mar 6, 2026) — the cleanest *bounded* loop shipped by an individual: the agent may edit **only** `train.py`; the human owns `program.md`; the evaluator lives in a locked `prepare.py` it cannot touch ("the agent cannot quietly change the evaluation to make itself look good"); each experiment gets a fixed 5-minute budget; improvement on `val_bpb` → git-keep, otherwise git-revert. First overnight run: ~83 experiments, 15 kept, val_bpb ~1.000 → 0.975. It is a research toy for ML training — no business, no forecasts, no meta-grading — but its *separation of the scoring instrument from the self-modifier* is the design principle Caps. 2–5 presuppose, demonstrated in public by one person.
- **Memento-Skills** (Zhou et al., multi-university, arXiv 2603.18743, Mar 19, 2026) — skills as structured markdown, evolved by a read–write reflective loop without touching model weights: GAIA test accuracy 66.0% vs 52.3% for the no-optimisation ablation; Humanity's Last Exam 38.7% vs 17.9%. Benchmarks only; code public.
- **Voyager** (Wang et al., 2023, NVIDIA/university — borderline lab-affiliated, listed for lineage) — Minecraft agent with an ever-growing executable skill library, automatic curriculum and self-verification (3.3× more unique items). The ancestor of every skill-evolution system above, Hermes included.
- **STOP** (Zelikman et al., 2023) and **ADAS / Meta Agent Search** (Hu et al., 2024) — scaffold self-improvement and meta-agent search over agent designs; authors of STOP explicitly note the fixed LLM means it is not full recursive self-improvement.

**Verdict on (e):** non-lab academia has demonstrated recursive self-improvement *against fixed, mechanical evaluators in sandboxes*. The step to production-like settings — where the evaluator is business outcomes, the changes carry money/customer risk, and the evaluator itself must be graded — is taken by **none** of them.

### 7. Closest non-lab analogues — where each stops short or exceeds

1. **Felix (closest overall).** *Matches/exceeds:* real, verified revenue at a scale (~$195k in ~7 weeks) the reference system's public description does not claim to exceed; genuine sub-agent operations across sales/support/content; a nightly self-improvement loop keyed to *where the human had to intervene* — an elegant autonomy-expansion signal. *Stops short:* no pre-registered forecasts, no scoring of its own predictions, no meta-grading, no twin certification, no formal adoption rule (its nightly reprogramming is unbounded and un-audited in any published record), no reconciled-books proof job, no independent audit; revenue is circular (selling agent setups) and media-driven; the human is in the loop daily.
2. **Karpathy's autoresearch (closest on bounded self-modification design).** *Exceeds in one respect:* its evaluator-separation (locked `prepare.py`) is a cleaner anti-self-deception boundary than most production systems document. *Stops short:* it improves a training script against one static metric; no business, no outcome-settled parameter adoption, no forecasting or meta-evaluation at all.
3. **Hermes Agent (closest on auditable self-modification in a shipping product).** Hash-logged, reversible skill mutation in production use. *Stops short:* adoption is ungated by default, skills are not settled against business outcomes, and no business run is documented.
4. **SICA / Gödel Agent (closest on validated recursive self-improvement).** Empirically validated self-rewrites with overseers — methodologically stronger self-modification than Felix's. *Stops short:* entirely benchmark-bound; the "outcomes" are test scores, the "risk" is API spend.
5. **Polsia (closest on claimed scale).** If its claims held, it would exceed the reference on Cap. 1 breadth (thousands of companies). *Stops short:* claims are unverified, partly unverifiable as presented, contradicted in degree by user-review signals, and none of Caps. 2–7 is even claimed in documented form.
6. **Andon Vending-Bench (closest on the synthetic-twin idea).** A serious synthetic business environment. *Stops short:* it certifies *models*, externally — no system uses it (or anything like it) to certify its own evaluators against a planted defect.

### 8. Lab context markers (separated — NOT part of the non-lab survey)

- **Anthropic / Andon Labs, Project Vend (2025):** Claude "Claudius" ran a real office vending business; Anthropic's own verdict on Phase 1 was "we would not hire Claudius" (losses, hallucinated Venmo, identity episode); Phase 2 (better model + a supervisor agent, "Seymour," who cut unauthorised discounts ~80%) reached a modest profit by Dec 2025 across SF/NYC/London machines. Even the lab system had **no forecast ledger, no meta-grading, no twin certification, no self-modification adoption rule** — its improvement between phases was human-engineered.
- **Sakana AI, Darwin Gödel Machine (May 2025; ICLR 2026):** archive-based self-modifying coding agent, SWE-bench 20.0% → 50.0%, Polyglot 14.2% → 30.7%, empirically validated on benchmarks, sandboxed, with documented reward-hacking incidents. Benchmark-only.
- **Meta et al., HyperAgents / DGM-H (arXiv 2603.19461, Mar 2026):** the meta-agent itself becomes editable and meta-improvements transfer across domains (coding, paper review, robotics reward design, math grading) — the deepest published self-modification, with the explicit boundary that the **outer selection/evaluation process is not editable** and all experiments sandboxed with human oversight.
These three mark what the labs have: deeper self-modification research (Sakana/Meta) and one real-business experiment (Anthropic) — **still not the reference combination**, which is precisely what makes the reference system's accountability stack (Caps. 2–4, 6) unusual even by lab standards, on the public record.
